On Arrakis, a storm rarely arrives all at once. The wind shifts, the air thickens, a dark line rises on the horizon, and people who live in the desert know that by then, they should already have chosen their shelter.

Arrakis is the desert planet of *Dune*, Frank Herbert's 1965 novel. The whole economy of the Empire depends on a single resource there, the spice, extracted at great cost under the constant threat of storms. I often think of it when I look at AI: an entire economy hanging on one scarce, expensive resource, compute, and companies investing fortunes on the bet that the sky will stay clear.

This week had that color. On October 29, Alphabet lost 12.6%. The next day, Intel fell almost 30% and the Nasdaq more than 3%. That same Friday morning, Insee announced that French GDP had shrunk. None of this is a bubble bursting yet. These are gusts, and they tell you which way the wind is blowing.

> **In brief**
>
> - **What:** a sharp correction in AI stocks on October 29 and 30, 2026, triggered by results from Alphabet, Amazon and Intel. Not yet a bubble bursting.
> - **Key figures:** Alphabet -12.6% in one session and -$9.1 billion of free cash flow in Q3. Intel down as much as 30%, its worst day since 1974. Nasdaq -3% on October 30.
> - **What the market punished:** the cost of AI. Demand keeps growing (Google Cloud +103%, AWS +39%, Azure +48%).
> - **The most worrying sign:** credit. CoreWeave and Oracle are paying for their debt at the price of fragile companies.
> - **In France:** GDP -0.1% in Q3, unemployment at 8.3%, 10-year OAT yield above 5%. And Capgemini is cutting 2,409 jobs in France while growing worldwide.

## The wind shifts

The first signs came from the debt market, which few people watch. In mid-October, the cost of insuring against a default by CoreWeave, which rents computing power to AI companies, went above 1,100 basis points. That is distressed territory. Its bonds due 2032 traded around 77 cents on the dollar. Oracle's, which builds data centers for OpenAI, hit a record 288 basis points on October 15. An Evercore ISI trader quoted by [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/coreweave-credit-default-swaps-hit-distress-levels-as-investors-sour-on-ai-debt.html) put it in two words: the market is saying "no más" to AI debt without operating cash flow behind it.

Elsewhere, the storm has already passed. In China, the STAR50 AI index has lost about 40% since its July peak, wiping out some $480 billion according to [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/ai-bubble-bursts-china-wiping-195630824.html). In Frankfurt, the ECB has listed an AI valuation correction among the main risks to the euro area since May, and five of its economists consider it likely even if the technology delivers.

At the center of it all is compute, the spice of this economy. According to estimates in circulation, Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta will spend between $725 and $760 billion on capital expenditure this year. Nvidia is financing up to $105 billion for an OpenAI data center in Ohio, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/17/nvidia-financing-open-ai-data-center-ohio.html). The supplier lends to its customer so the customer can buy its product. That kind of arrangement holds up fine as long as money keeps flowing. The bill is physical too: data centers used 448 TWh in 2025 and are projected to reach 945 TWh in 2030, roughly Japan's consumption, as I detailed in [my piece on AI's ecological footprint](/en/ai-ecology-2026-prompt-infrastructure-en/).

## The first gusts

They came with quarterly earnings. On the evening of October 28, Alphabet reported 103% growth at Google Cloud. But the group spent $51.4 billion on capital expenditure in the quarter, leaving free cash flow at -$9.1 billion when analysts expected around -$2.7 billion. The 2026 budget rises to $195-205 billion, and management expects a "significant" increase in 2027. The next day, the stock lost 12.6% and roughly $500 billion in value, [its worst session](https://fortune.com/2026/10/28/alphabet-google-stock-drops-cloud-revenue-doubles-free-cash-flow-negative/) according to several outlets.

![Cloud growth in Q3 2026: Google Cloud +103%, Microsoft Azure +48%, AWS +39%](/assets/img/ai-bubble-cloud-growth.svg)

Amazon [followed on the evening of the 29th](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/aws-q3-earnings-report-2026.html). AWS posted its fastest growth since late 2021, at 39%, with a $578 billion backlog. But trailing twelve-month free cash flow fell to -$42.4 billion, and long-term debt went from $65.6 to $173.1 billion in nine months. The stock fell 6 to 7% after the close.

Intel, on the 30th, is a separate case. Its collapse, which [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/29/intel-intc-q3-earnings-report-2026-.html) tied to the PC slowdown, also stems from a manufacturing incident, with no direct link to AI. In this climate, the market didn't sort things out.

Microsoft was spared. Azure grew 48%, capital spending came in below what the company had guided, and free cash flow beat expectations by $10 billion. The stock rose as much as 4% after the release. The gusts hit those who pay for AI without being able to show the return yet.

![Stock moves in late October 2026: Intel down as much as 30%, Alphabet down 12.6%, Amazon down about 7%, Microsoft up as much as 4%](/assets/img/ai-bubble-market-reaction.svg)

Last year, I wrote about [Sam Altman's statements on the bubble](/en/sam-altman-ai-bubble-markets-analysis/). The OpenAI CEO spoke of a "grain of truth" that gets smart people overexcited. The grain of truth is still there, as cloud growth rates show. Its price is what worries people.

## What would be swept away

The S&P 500 is only a few percent off its record, and most analysts quoted by [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/ai-stocks-selloff-ai-spending-returns.html) call this a correction like others before it. For the real storm to arrive, funding would have to dry up: a heavily indebted player unable to refinance, an OpenAI partner pulling out, or a giant announcing it is cutting capital spending. A bubble usually bursts when money stops flowing.

If it came, it would first take whatever has no foundations: compute providers financed on credit, and hardware suppliers whose entire growth depends on data centers. It would also hit Anthropic's IPO, expected around November 16, whose [S-1 I went through](/en/anthropic-ipo-s1-filing-en/) this week. Listing on the Nasdaq in this climate will be a test.

## France, exposed

France goes into this period with few reserves. On October 30, [Insee](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/9111036) reported that GDP fell 0.1% in the third quarter, after stagnating in the second. Economists had expected +0.1%. Exports fell 1.7%, investment 0.6%, and growth already locked in for 2026 is down to 0.2%. Unemployment stood at 8.3% in the second quarter.

Borrowing is also getting more expensive. The 10-year OAT yield crossed 5% in October for the first time since 2002 and reached 5.14% on October 22, according to [Boursorama](https://www.boursorama.com/bourse/actualites/taux-france-le-rendement-de-l-oat-a-dix-ans-grimpe-a-5-14-actualise-784f4f19fe052c6a20a06ce358d9c9c3). The spread over Germany went above 160 basis points. Moody's kept its Aa3 rating with a negative outlook, but doubts a compromise can be reached on the budget and its €43 billion of savings. Oil, pushed by the conflict between the United States and Iran, trades above $110 a barrel for Brent.

[ECB economists](https://www.daf-mag.fr/bi-1244/transformation-processus-2133/essor-de-lia-la-bce-redoute-une-correction-des-marches-financiers-26964) point out that Europe has less fiscal and monetary room than in 2000 to absorb a shock coming from US tech. A country borrowing at 5% with a deficit around 5% of GDP is exactly what they describe. For France, the danger would be an AI correction pushing investors away from anything that looks fragile, French government debt included.

The country does hold some cards. Mistral AI took on $830 million of debt, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/30/mistral-ai-raises-830m-in-debt-to-set-up-a-data-center-near-paris/), to build a data center in Bruyères-le-Châtel. In May, before the National Assembly, Arthur Mensch pointed to nuclear power as an asset for AI, and warned that importing AI could cost Europe a trillion euros a year within three to four years, added to the trade deficit. I [analyzed that hearing](/en/arthur-mensch-mistral-ai-national-assembly-hearing-en/). If credit to AI tightened, these debt-financed projects would feel it too.

The Paris stock exchange ended October almost flat. AI hardware names fell (STMicroelectronics, Schneider Electric, Legrand), while IT services stocks rose.

## The ground after

A storm moves things as much as it destroys them. When the wind drops, the terrain has changed. That is already happening to tech jobs, before the storm is even here.

On October 30, Capgemini [reported](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/30/3392023/0/fr/la-dynamique-du-3%C3%A8me-trimestre-2026-conduit-capgemini-%C3%A0-relever-ses-perspectives-2026.html) quarterly revenue of €6.07 billion, up 11.7% at constant exchange rates, and raised its full-year target. The group has 417,500 employees, 18% more than a year ago, mostly thanks to the acquisition of WNS, which is largely based in India. In France, it signed a collective voluntary departure agreement in May covering 2,409 jobs, about 7% of its French workforce. One figure goes almost unnoticed: bookings grew only 3.4%, three times slower than revenue.

![Capgemini in Q3 2026: revenue +11.7%, bookings +3.4%, group headcount +18%](/assets/img/ai-bubble-capgemini.svg)

The rest of the sector is moving cautiously. Numeum, the French digital industry body, [forecasts 3% growth](https://www.lemondeinformatique.fr/actualites/lire-numeum-prevoit-3-de-croissance-du-marche-numerique-en-2026-100693.html) for the French digital market in 2026, but only 1.4% for IT services firms, and 35% of them expect lower margins. Sopra Steria posted 4.3% organic growth in the third quarter.

The youngest feel the ground move the most. According to the [2026 Numeum-KPMG survey](https://kpmg.com/fr/fr/media/press-releases/2026/10/esn-ict-etude-ia-2026.html), 62% of French IT services firms say AI is already weighing on their hiring, 47% require AI skills from the start of a career, and 22% see junior hiring falling sharply, versus 9% rising. In the United States, a Stanford study based on ADP payroll data found employment 19% lower in June 2026 among 22-25 year-olds in AI-exposed jobs. According to Indeed, 71% of the increase in developer job postings between May 2025 and May 2026 was for senior roles. The French executive recruitment agency Apec still forecasts about 61,000 IT executive hires in 2026, up 4%, but those projections predate the negative GDP figure.

![Numeum-KPMG 2026 survey: 22% of IT services firms expect junior hiring to fall sharply, 9% expect it to rise](/assets/img/ai-bubble-it-services-hiring.svg)

A market correction would speed this up. Companies under pressure look for savings, and AI is the easiest promise to sell to a board. In [my analysis of whether AI will replace developers](/en/will-ai-replace-developers-critical-analysis/), I noted that AI excels at repetitive tasks, scaffolding, test generation and documentation, and that the profession is shifting toward architecture. Those are exactly the tasks juniors used to learn on. In his hearing, Arthur Mensch said AI consumption already amounted to 10% of Mistral's payroll. Behind that ratio, there are jobs.

The profiles that hold are the ones with foundations: architects, tech leads, security and cloud specialists, and people who know how to orchestrate models instead of competing with them. The [Forward Deployed Engineer](/en/forward-deployed-engineer-fde-role-en/) role, which AI vendors are hiring by the hundreds to plug their models into customers' systems, is one example. So are the teams industrializing their work with agents, which is what I tried to formalize with [Kaizen, a full development lifecycle for Claude Code](/en/kaizen-ai-sdlc-claude-code-en/).

## What I'm watching

November 3, the US midterm elections, with the risk of escalation with Iran right after. Around November 16, Anthropic's IPO. November 17, Nvidia's earnings. November 27, the detailed French GDP figures, with the budget vote and the spread over Germany in the background.

And above all, two signals worth more than any trading session: a tech giant cutting its capital spending, or an indebted player like CoreWeave failing to refinance. If either shows up, it will no longer be gusts.
